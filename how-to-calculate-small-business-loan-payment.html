How to Calculate Your Small Business Loan Payment (Before You Sign)

By MarginWize Editorial Team • August 2026 • 6 Min Read

A lender approves you for $150,000 at 9% interest over 7 years. Before you sign anything, there's one number you need to calculate yourself: your actual monthly payment. Lenders will quote it to you, but understanding how that figure is calculated — and what happens when any single variable changes — is what separates business owners who negotiate confidently from those who accept the first number they're handed.

The Loan Payment Formula

Small business loans typically use an amortized payment structure, meaning you pay the same fixed amount every month, with each payment split between interest and principal. Early payments are mostly interest; later payments are mostly principal.

The Monthly Payment Formula:
M = P × [r(1 + r)n] / [(1 + r)n − 1]

Where:

  • M = Monthly payment
  • P = Principal (the total loan amount borrowed)
  • r = Monthly interest rate (annual interest rate ÷ 12)
  • n = Total number of payments (loan term in years × 12)

This formula looks intimidating, but the logic behind it is simple: it spreads the total cost of borrowing evenly across every payment, so the dollar amount never changes even though the interest-to-principal ratio shifts every month.

A Real Example

Take that $150,000 loan at 9% annual interest over 7 years:

• P = $150,000
• r = 9% ÷ 12 = 0.0075 (monthly rate)
• n = 7 × 12 = 84 payments

Running this through the formula gives a monthly payment of approximately $2,377. Over the full 7-year term, that's a total of about $199,668 paid — meaning roughly $49,668 in interest on top of the original $150,000 borrowed.

Monthly Payment ~$2,377
Total Loan Cost ~$199,668
Total Interest Paid ~$49,668

That total interest figure is the number most business owners underestimate until they see it laid out. A loan that "costs 9%" doesn't cost 9% of the principal total — it costs 9% annually on the remaining balance, compounding over the full term, which adds up to a much larger total than the headline rate suggests.

How the Interest Rate Changes Everything

Small shifts in interest rate produce larger shifts in total cost than most borrowers expect, because the effect compounds over the full loan term. On that same $150,000 loan over 7 years:

At 7% Interest Competitive bank / SBA terms
$2,277/mo • Total Interest: $41,268
At 9% Interest (Base Example) Standard commercial rate
$2,377/mo • Total Interest: $49,668
At 11% Interest Higher-risk / alternative lender
$2,479/mo • Total Interest: $58,436

A 4-percentage-point difference in rate — which can easily separate one lender's offer from another's — adds roughly $17,000 in total interest over the life of this loan. This is exactly why shopping multiple lenders before signing isn't just good practice; on a loan this size, it's the difference between two very different total costs for the same borrowed amount.

SBA Loans vs. Standard Term Loans: What Actually Differs

SBA loans (backed by the U.S. Small Business Administration) are one of the most searched loan types for small business owners, and they work slightly differently from a standard bank term loan:

1 SBA 7(a) Loans

SBA 7(a) loans are the most common SBA loan type, typically offering longer repayment terms (up to 10 years for working capital, up to 25 years for real estate) and lower down payment requirements than conventional loans. The tradeoff is a longer, more document-heavy approval process, often taking several weeks to a few months.

2 Standard Bank Term Loans

Standard term loans from banks or online lenders usually close faster — sometimes within days — but often come with shorter repayment terms and, depending on your credit profile, higher interest rates than an SBA-backed loan.

3 Business Lines of Credit

Business lines of credit work differently from both: instead of a lump sum with a fixed payment schedule, you're approved for a maximum credit limit and only pay interest on what you actually draw, similar to a credit card. This makes them useful for managing cash flow gaps rather than funding a single large purchase.

What's a "Normal" Interest Rate for a Small Business Loan?

This varies significantly by lender type, your credit profile, and current market rates, but as a general reference: SBA loans often carry some of the more competitive rates available to small businesses specifically because they're partially government-backed, reducing lender risk. Online and alternative lenders, which typically offer faster funding and more lenient approval criteria, generally charge meaningfully higher rates to offset that increased risk and speed. Traditional bank term loans usually fall somewhere between the two, depending heavily on the strength of your business's financials and time in operation.

Questions to Ask Before You Sign

Beyond the headline interest rate, a few details change your real monthly payment and total cost significantly:

  • •
    Is the rate fixed or variable? A variable rate loan can see its monthly payment increase mid-term if benchmark rates rise.
  • •
    Are there origination fees? A 2-3% origination fee on a $150,000 loan adds $3,000–$4,500 upfront, effectively increasing your real borrowing cost beyond the quoted interest rate.
  • •
    Is there a prepayment penalty? Some loans charge a fee if you pay off the balance early, which matters if you expect strong revenue growth and might want to clear the debt faster than scheduled.

Bottom Line

The monthly payment a lender quotes you is a single number built from several moving parts — principal, rate, and term — and understanding how each one affects your total cost puts you in a stronger position to compare offers and negotiate, rather than simply accepting the first approval you receive. Even a few percentage points of difference in rate, or a few years of difference in term length, can shift your total interest cost by tens of thousands of dollars over the life of a loan.
